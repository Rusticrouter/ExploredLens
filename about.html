The Photographer

Craig

Craig has spent a lifetime looking. Not just through a viewfinder, but at the world the way most people never slow down enough to see — the short-eared owl banking low over a winter marsh at dusk, the way a couple's hands find each other without thinking, the last light burning gold through a New England tree line before it's gone.

Origins

That curiosity showed up early. A camera found its way into his hands young, and what started as childhood wonder never really left. It just got sharper — along with the lenses.

The Work

Based on the Seacoast of New Hampshire, Craig works across three disciplines that share a single thread: presence. Wildlife photography demands it — you either wait for the moment or you miss it forever. Portrait work rewards it — people relax when they feel genuinely seen. And landscape photography requires it — the land doesn't perform on schedule, and the light doesn't wait.

Recognition

His wildlife photography has been recognized by Audubon, one of conservation photography's most respected voices. But the work that means the most to him rarely ends up in a magazine. It's the family portrait that becomes a centerpiece above someone's fireplace. The owl photo that gets framed above a desk. The engagement session that captures a couple exactly as they are — before everything changes.

The Approach

Every session, every frame, is approached the same way — with patience, genuine curiosity, and a deep respect for what's in front of the lens. Whether that's a wild creature that might disappear at any moment, or a family that's never quite this age again.

Craig shoots with professional Nikon gear built for exceptional detail, sharpness, and color — because imagery this personal deserves to be printed large and last a lifetime. His background in wildlife photography also translates directly into portrait work: comfort in fast-changing conditions, an instinct for finding great natural light, and a calm presence that puts people at ease even when the camera is close.
